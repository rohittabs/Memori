So why does cramming fail?

Read a fact 6 times tonight, and it is gone by Friday.

Space those same 6 reviews out, and you keep it for years.

Same effort. The difference is WHEN.

Think of a leaky bucket

Your memory of a fact is a bucket with a small hole.

Top it up just before it runs empty, and two things happen: it refills, and the hole gets smaller.

That is spaced repetition.

Rule 1

Be honest

The app cannot see you. It only knows which button you pressed.

Press "Good" on something you barely dragged out of your memory, and it will hide that card for 50 days.

Trick: say the answer out loud before you flip.

Rule 2

One card, one fact

Bad
"Explain the circle of fifths"
Good
"How many sharps are in D major?" then "Two"

If a card takes you more than 15 seconds, split it up.

Rule 3

A little, every day

10 minutes every day beats 2 hours every Sunday.

The whole system is built on daily gaps. Skipping days creates a pile-up, and a pile-up makes you quit.

Rule 4

Go slow with new cards

Every new card you add today creates roughly 8 to 10 reviews over the next year.

20 new cards a day becomes about 200 reviews a day once it settles.

That is why the limit exists. It protects future you.
